Board design notes

  • Results are never pre-decided. Each drop gets a tiny random start offset, spin and bounce variation from crypto.getRandomValues; physics decides the slot. Nothing looks at the bet or the prize.
  • Wide, bouncy board. Pegs are 60 apart (about 3 ball widths) in 8 rows, about 44 pegs in all. Each bounce moves the ball half a column, so after 8 rows every hole spreads over all 5 slots. Holes stay within about 1% of each other even with holes over the side slots.
  • Jackpot diamond. A 48 × 24 diamond replaces the centre peg in the middle of the board. It leaves 29 units to the neighbouring pegs; the earlier 60-wide one left 23, and balls rattled in that gap. The rows below re-mix the balls, so the jackpot stays at about 19% and every hole pays the same.
  • Diamond bonus bar. Each bounce off the diamond gives points equal to the bet. Contacts less than 0.12 s apart count as one bounce, so a ball rolling along the diamond isn't counted many times. A ball bounces off it 0.61 times on average from every hole, so the bar never favours one hole. At 11 points per Skip'it, a full 100-ball event gives about 5 Skip'its at any bet. Bigger bets make it swingier: always-x1 players get 4–6, always-x10 players get 1–10.
  • No wall sliding. Large half-round bumpers on alternate rows push balls back into the field.
  • Slow, readable drop. Low gravity gives about 5.7 s per drop at 1×, with visible bounces. The 2× button changes speed only, never the odds.
  • Peg gaps are over twice the ball's width, so balls never wedge. A stuck-ball nudge still kicks in after 0.4 s just in case.
  • The slingshot can't miss. The aim snaps to the nearest hole, and pull strength only changes flight speed. Skill can't change the odds.
  • The stake is always visible. Slot labels show the actual payout for the chosen bet and pass (e.g. 2.5K at x10). While aiming the slingshot, a small prize strip under the sign shows the same values. It is offset and narrower than the hole row, so no hole looks tied to one prize.
  • Bet only scales the prize. One x10 ball pays the same on average as ten x1 balls. It only adds variance and saves time.
